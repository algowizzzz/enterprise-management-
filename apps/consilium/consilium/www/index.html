Using the system

Setting up a governance forum

A forum is never created directly. You raise a formation request; it is evaluated against the governance criteria; and an approved request is what brings the forum into existence. That order exists so every forum on the inventory has a recorded reason for being there and a named sponsor.

  1. Search the inventory first. Most refused requests duplicate a forum that already covers the ground.
  2. Choose the type. The type settles how much authority the forum may hold and who it answers to.
  3. Name the accountable line. One operating group owns the forum; a line of business may sit beneath it.
  4. Write the mandate and the escalation route. A forum with no route out of it quietly absorbs risk.
  5. Expect a compliance review. Nothing is approved without one.

Forum types, and choosing between them

Work down from authority. Ask what the forum may decide on its own, and the type follows. Where two types would fit, take the one with less authority: authority is easier to add at the next review than to withdraw.

Templates you can start from

Templates fix the shape of a document or an escalation so that reviewers are not reading a different structure every time. A template names the fields a submission must carry and, where one is held, the file to start from. Use the template that matches the action you are taking — issuing a new document, revising one, reviewing one or retiring one — rather than the nearest document you happen to have.

Where decision authority sits

Authority travels downward and is always narrower than the authority above it. The board holds what it has reserved to itself and delegates the rest; a board committee acts within that delegation; an executive committee acts within the delegation it has been given by the board or the chief executive; a management committee acts within its business. Councils, advisory forums and working groups recommend — they do not decide.

Three things settle whether a decision stands: the forum holds the authority for it, the sitting was quorate under the forum's own quorum rule, and the decision is recorded as a motion with its outcome. Where any of the three is missing, the decision has not been taken, however firmly it was agreed in the room.

A forum's own page shows its quorum rule, its voting seats and the motions it has carried.

Escalation protocols

Every forum records two things about escalation: the threshold that triggers it, and the protocol that says where the matter goes and how quickly.

A threshold is a measurable condition rather than a judgement — a limit breached, a severity rating reached, a regulatory deadline missed. A protocol names the receiving forum, the timescale, and what must travel with the matter: the decision being sought, the accountable owner, and the treatment already attempted.

Matters normally travel up the parent chain. They move sideways only where an escalation link between the two forums has been recorded, which is what the interconnectivity view on each forum shows.
