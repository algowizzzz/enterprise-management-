# User guide

For everyone who uses the portal: committee secretaries, forum owners, policy
owners and reviewers, escalation owners, the governance office, and anyone who
only needs to look something up.

The portal has three areas, and every record in it has an owner, a history and
a next step:

| Area | What it holds |
|---|---|
| **Forums** | The governance forums (boards, committees, councils, working groups): who sits on them, when they meet, what they decided, and whether they are compliant. |
| **Policies** | The governing documents (frameworks, policies, standards, procedures) and where each one is in its lifecycle. |
| **Escalations** | Matters raised because something needs attention: who owns them, how serious they are, how late they are, and how they were resolved. |

There are two ways into the system. Most people only ever need the first.

- **The portal** is the address the system opens on. It shows the inventories,
  the dashboards and the guided forms.
- **The workspace** (`/app`) is the full set of record screens. People who
  maintain records use it, and the portal links into it wherever you need to
  edit something the portal only shows.

---

## 1. Signing in and finding your way

Open the portal's address and sign in with your work account. If you open a
page while signed out, the page asks you to sign in and returns you to it
afterwards.

The bar across the top holds:

- **The tabs:** Home, Forums, Policies, Escalations, Reports and Admin. The
  governance office also sees **Requests**. You only see the Admin tab's
  contents if you hold an administrator role.
- **Text size (A− / A+ / ↺).** This changes the size of all text on every
  page, and is remembered on this browser.
- **Light / dark theme (☾).** Also remembered.
- **Your name.** This opens a menu with your profile and **Sign out**.

**What you can see depends on your roles.** A screen you have no access to
tells you so rather than showing it empty. Some escalations are marked
**sensitive**. If you do not hold the access role for them, they do not appear
anywhere: not in lists, counts, search or reports. If you follow a link to one,
it reads the same as a reference that does not exist.

**Dates and times.** A date (a due date, a review date) is a calendar day. A
time is shown in your own time zone, with the zone named, for example
"10:15 PM CDT".

## 2. Home

The home page opens with the portal's banner and two buttons for the most
common tasks. Below the banner:

- **Quick links** to Forums, Policies, Escalations and Reports.
- **Where the inventory stands:** active forums, forums awaiting a compliance
  review, reviews overdue, and formation requests still open. Each figure
  opens the matching filtered list.
- **Forums by category** and **Compliance standing:** counts by type, risk
  category and status.
- **Recent changes:** the forums whose records changed most recently.
- **Using the system:** guidance written by the governance office. It is
  maintained in the system, so it may have been updated since you last read it.

## 3. Forums

### Finding a forum

**Forums** lists every forum on the inventory. Narrow it with the filters
(type, compliance status, cadence, primary risk category, owning operating
group, and **Standing**: active, disbanded, awaiting review,
regulatory-required, review overdue). You can also type in the table's search
box, which searches names, references and mandates.

The filters are kept in the page address. Reloading the page, going back, or
sending the link to a colleague shows the same filtered list.

Click a row to open the forum.

### A forum's page

The header shows the forum's type, its compliance standing, its officers and
its next review date. The tabs hold everything else:

| Tab | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **Details** | The mandate, the full record, its scope (business units, risk types, legal entities, jurisdictions) and its escalation and quorum rules. |
| **Membership** | Who holds a seat. Change **Membership as at** to see who sat on the forum on any past date: seats are ended, never deleted, so the history is exact. |
| **Linkages** | The forum's parent, sub-forums and the forums it escalates to or informs, as a map and as a list. |
| **Documents** | The charter, and the governing documents this forum approves. |
| **Decisions** | Motions and their outcomes, and the meetings held. A decision only counts if the meeting was quorate and the outcome was recorded. |
| **History** | Every compliance decision about the forum, and every change to its record. |

**Edit this forum** opens the record in the workspace, if you may edit it.

### Asking for a new forum, or a change to one

A forum is never created directly. You raise a **formation request**, the
governance office evaluates it, and an approved request creates the forum.
That way every forum on the inventory has a recorded reason for existing and a
named sponsor.

1. **Search the inventory first.** The most common reason a request comes back
   is that a forum already covers the ground.
2. Choose **Request a new forum** (on Home or Forums).
3. Fill in the four sections: what you are asking for (create, modify or
   retire, and why), the forum, accountability, and timing. Fields marked \*
   are required. The three explanations need a few sentences each.
4. **Save draft** keeps your work so you can come back to it. The page address
   then includes the request's reference, so you can bookmark it.
5. **Submit for evaluation** hands the request to the governance office and
   locks it.

What happens next:

| Stage | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| **Submitted** | Waiting for the governance office to start. The system has already checked for overlap with existing forums. |
| **Under evaluation** | The office is assessing it against five criteria: the gap it fills, duplication, the escalation pathway, fit with the framework, and whether it can be resourced. |
| **Returned to you** | The office has questions. Open the request: the questions are shown with a box for your answer. Answering sends it back into evaluation. |
| **Pending approval** | The approval steps have been raised and each approver records a decision. |
| **Approved** | The forum has been created, in draft. It becomes compliant only after a compliance review. The request page links to it. |
| **Rejected / Withdrawn** | Closed, with the reason recorded. |

You can follow your request at any time from its page.

## 4. Policies

**Policies** lists every governing document. Filter by type, lifecycle phase,
owning operating group, risk category and handling classification. **Standing**
offers the useful cuts: in force, awaiting review, review overdue, review due
within 90 days, and no review date.

A document's page has five tabs:

| Tab | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **Details** | The document's record: owner, approver, classification, scope. |
| **Lifecycle** | Where it is in its lifecycle, which actions are available next, and anything that would block them. Also its approval chain and review cycles. |
| **Versions** | Every version, what changed and whether it was published. |
| **Lineage** | Its parent, children, addenda and what it supersedes. |
| **Horizon scanning** | The scans that assessed external change against it. |

### The lifecycle

`Draft → Review → Approved → Published → Implemented`, and `Retired` at the
end. Moves happen through the **Actions** menu on the document in the
workspace. Each move is limited to the role responsible for it:

| From | Action | To | Who |
|---|---|---|---|
| Draft | Submit for Review | Review | Policy Owner |
| Review | Record Approval | Approved | Enterprise Policy Office |
| Review | Return to Drafting | Draft | Policy Reviewer |
| Approved | Publish | Published | Enterprise Policy Office |
| Approved | Return to Drafting | Draft | Enterprise Policy Office |
| Published | Confirm Implementation | Implemented | Policy Owner |
| Published / Implemented | Reopen for Change | Draft | Policy Owner |
| Published / Implemented | Retire | Retired | Enterprise Policy Office |
| Retired | Reinstate | Draft | Enterprise Policy Office |

A move can be refused by a **lifecycle gate**, for example publishing without a
complete approval chain. The Lifecycle tab says what is missing. Every move is
recorded in the document's history.

## 5. Escalations

**Escalations** is the register of matters raised. Filter by open or closed,
severity, status, type, organisational level and age. There are also filters
for matters past their time limit.

### Raising an escalation

**Raise an escalation** opens a new matter in the workspace. You will need:
a title and description, the escalation type, the date it was identified, the
tier 1 risk type (and tier 2 if known), at least one impacted entity, who
identified it, the organisational level, the accountable executive, what
triggered it, and the severity. **Opened on** fills itself in.

**Severity source** decides who sets the severity. *Matrix* lets the escalation
matrix work it out from the matter's facts. *Manual override* keeps the
severity you chose.

### Working an escalation

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Open** | Raised; nobody has started on it. |
| **In progress** | Being worked. Add **action plans**, each with an owner and dates. |
| **Under review / Pending review** | With the second line for challenge. |
| **Closed** | Resolved, with its outcome recorded. |
| **Closed — tracked externally** | Handed to another system, with that system's reference. |

A matter's page shows its details, impacted entities and forums, action plans
and risk acceptances, reviews, closure and history.

**Deciding to accept a risk** rather than fix it is recorded as a **risk
acceptance**. It has a rationale, an accountable executive, start and end dates
and an approval. It expires, and is reassessed periodically.

**Closing a matter** takes two steps, because a matter must not close without
its outcome:

1. Record an **Escalation Closure**: the closure type (resolved, risk accepted,
   transferred externally, no action required) and a summary.
2. Tick **Response template completed** on the matter and set its status to
   **Closed**.

The system refuses to close a matter until both are done.

## 6. Reports

**Reports** is the management view across all three areas:

- Headline figures.
- Breakdowns of forums, documents and escalations.
- Records missing required information.
- Time-limit clocks.
- An escalation analysis you can group by month, quarter or year and
  **download as CSV**.

Every figure that has a matching list links to it, already filtered. Counts
only include records you are allowed to see.

## 7. For the governance office

The **Requests** tab is your queue of formation requests. **Whose move it is**
filters it to the requests waiting on you, on the originator, or on an
approver. The queue also lists forums awaiting a compliance review.

Opening a request shows only the actions that are valid at its current stage,
and each action asks you to confirm:

- **Start evaluation**, then record a finding and a note against each of the
  five criteria.
- **Return to originator** with your questions.
- **Raise approval steps.** The steps come from the formation approval route
  that has been configured.
- **Approve.** This is disabled until nothing blocks it, and it lists what is
  still outstanding: an unassessed criterion, an undecided step, or a step that
  was rejected.
- **Reject** or **Withdraw**, with a reason.
- **Raise an exception** when a step cannot be completed normally. Only the
  Head of Risk Governance can resolve it, or authorise a bypass.

**Recording a compliance review** (Compliance Reviewer role): open the forum and
choose **Record a compliance review**. Each decision says where it leaves the
forum: Compliant, Non-Compliant (you must give your reasons), Not Applicable, or
Returned to creator. A review is its own record and cannot be edited
afterwards. A later change to a watched field on the forum, such as its
mandate, sends it back for review automatically.

## 8. Roles at a glance

Your administrator assigns roles. What each one lets you do:

| Role | Can |
|---|---|
| Governance Viewer | Read forums, their membership and decisions. |
| Committee Secretary | Maintain forums they support: meetings, motions, votes, membership, charters. Raise formation requests. |
| Forum Owner | Maintain the forums they own. |
| Risk Governance Office | Run the forum inventory: evaluate formation requests, maintain forums, membership, meetings, charters, disbandment. |
| Head of Risk Governance | Everything the office can do, plus resolve exceptions and authorise bypasses. |
| Compliance Reviewer | Record compliance reviews on forums. |
| Policy Owner | Draft and maintain governing documents, intake requests, review cycles, monitoring, horizon scans, violations. |
| Policy Reviewer | Review documents and return them to drafting. |
| Enterprise Policy Office | Approve, publish, retire and reinstate documents; maintain templates, approval routes and lifecycle gates. |
| Escalation Owner | Raise and work escalations, action plans, risk acceptances, closures and periodic returns. |
| Escalation Reviewer | Challenge and review escalations. |
| Records Manager | Retention classes, legal holds, archiving and disposal. |
| Taxonomy Administrator | Maintain the reference lists (risk categories and types, organisation units, legal entities, forum types and roles, and the rest). |
| Consilium Audit | Read everything; change nothing. |
| Consilium Administrator | Configure the platform. |

## 9. Getting help

- **Something is missing or you cannot open it:** ask your administrator to
  check your roles. Record-level restrictions can also limit you to particular
  organisational units or legal entities.
- **A page looks wrong after an upgrade:** reload it. The portal fetches
  updated files automatically, but a page left open across an upgrade keeps
  the old ones until it is reloaded.
- **A save is refused:** the message says which rule refused it and why. Every
  refusal is also logged for the governance office.

## 10. Help assistant

Every page has a **Help** button in the bottom-right corner. It opens a panel
where you can ask about the page you are on, in your own words. It works from
this guide, the glossary, the governance office's guidance and a description
of every page, and it knows your roles, so its answers are about what *you*
can do.

Good questions to ask:

- **"What can I do here?"** What the page is for, and what you can and cannot
  do on it. On a record's page (a forum, a document, a request, a matter) it
  also says where the record stands and which actions are open to you now.
- **"How do I …?"** The steps, and a link to where you do it. If your roles do
  not allow it, it says so and names the role it needs.
- **"Why can't I …?"** Whether the reason is a role you do not hold, the
  record's current stage, or something that has to be done first.
- **"Where do I find …?"** A link to the right page.
- **"What does … mean?"** The glossary definition.

Each answer can show the guide sections it drew on (**From the guides**), with
links to where they apply, and suggests what to ask or open next.

**What it will not do.** It never tells you anything about a record you
cannot open. A reference you have no access to and a reference that does not
exist get the same answer. It does not change anything: every action is still
taken on the page itself, which checks your access again.

**Your conversation** stays in this browser tab until you close it or choose
the bin icon. Questions are recorded, with the page they were asked on, so
the governance office can see what people need help with. You can ask up to
30 questions an hour (your administrator may set a different limit).

Your administrator may have connected an AI service to phrase the answers.
The panel then says **AI-assisted**; the answer is still built from the same
guides and your access, and if the service does not answer, the built-in
answer is shown instead.
